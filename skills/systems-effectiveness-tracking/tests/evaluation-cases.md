# Systems Effectiveness Tracking — evaluation cases

Use these cases whenever the skill changes.

## 1. Outputs are not outcomes

Input:
> The programme conducted 120 trainings and therefore achieved farmer adoption.

Pass criteria:
- Training count is treated as an output.
- Adoption requires separate evidence.
- The skill does not infer outcome from activity/output completion.

## 2. Programme success is not system improvement

Input:
> Participating farms reduced irrigation depth, so the watershed is now water secure.

Pass criteria:
- Farm-level intervention performance is separated from watershed-level condition.
- The skill asks whether adoption coverage and hydrologic response are sufficient to affect the wider system.
- It does not claim watershed water security from farm evidence alone.

## 3. Wet-year / dry-year effect

Input:
> The intervention was evaluated only in a wet year. The programme claims that it is resilient to drought.

Pass criteria:
- The claim is not accepted.
- The skill identifies climatic variability as a material evidence gap.
- It may recommend repeated or dry-year monitoring without inventing results.

## 4. Seasonal timing mismatch

Input:
> Baseline and endline groundwater measurements were both collected in summer, while the expected intervention effect is improved monsoon recharge.

Pass criteria:
- The skill flags that project start/end timing may not capture the mechanism.
- It recommends a monitoring design aligned with the hydrologic cycle.
- It does not infer recharge impact from the available snapshots.

## 5. Continuous monitoring need

Input:
> The programme wants to assess groundwater response using one pre-project and one post-project measurement.

Pass criteria:
- The skill asks whether seasonal variability, pumping and rainfall require repeated/continuous monitoring.
- It does not require continuous sensors automatically where repeated field measurements could answer the question.
- The monitoring intensity is tied to the decision and mechanism.

## 6. Cost-per-output without benchmark

Input:
> A report cost ₹18 lakh to produce, so the programme is inefficient.

Pass criteria:
- The skill does not label inefficiency from the cost alone.
- It asks about scope, novelty, staff time, geography, quality requirements and comparable benchmarks.
- It may use the figure as an internal anomaly signal if context supports it.

## 7. Funding leverage attribution

Input:
> A ₹1 crore grant was followed by a ₹12 crore government allocation, so the programme reports 12× leverage.

Pass criteria:
- The skill asks whether the government allocation was causally influenced by the grant.
- It checks timing, other actors, deadweight and double-claiming.
- If attribution is weak, it treats leverage as a contribution/influence indicator rather than proven causation.

## 8. SROI not mandatory

Input:
> Every DSC programme must report one monetised SROI ratio.

Pass criteria:
- The skill does not impose SROI universally.
- It asks whether important outcomes can be meaningfully monetised and whether valuation assumptions are credible.
- Non-monetary outcome frameworks remain acceptable.

## 9. Rebound / Jevons risk

Input:
> Drip irrigation reduced water applied per irrigation, so total groundwater abstraction must have declined.

Pass criteria:
- The skill does not infer lower total abstraction automatically.
- It checks irrigation frequency, area expansion, crop changes and total pumping/use.
- Rebound risk is treated as a system-level consideration.

## 10. Zero-sum redistribution

Input:
> One user group increased its reliable water access, so the overall canal system improved.

Pass criteria:
- The skill checks whether gains came at the expense of downstream/other users.
- Distribution and equity are considered where material.
- Local benefit is not automatically reported as net system improvement.

## 11. Composite index hides trade-offs

Input:
> The water-security index increased from 62 to 68, while ecosystem health declined and access improved.

Pass criteria:
- The skill does not rely only on the composite score.
- It surfaces the opposing sub-dimension movements.
- It asks whether weights/normalisation are transparent before interpreting the aggregate.

## 12. Dashboard mixes levels

Input:
> The Board dashboard combines reserve days, number of trainings, adoption rate and groundwater trend into one programme score.

Pass criteria:
- The skill separates organisational health, programme output, intervention outcome and external system condition.
- It warns against a composite score that obscures these distinct questions.
- It recommends decision-oriented grouping.

## 13. Data system not operational

Input:
> The MEL plan lists 45 indicators, but there are no named data owners, definitions, validation rules, archive process or analysis responsibility.

Pass criteria:
- The skill does not call the framework operational.
- It flags data-system readiness as a material gap.
- It recommends ownership, metadata, QA, storage, analysis and reporting controls.

## 14. Funder stereotype safeguard

Input:
> This is a CSR donor, so assume they only care about output counts and branding.

Pass criteria:
- The skill rejects the stereotype as a design basis.
- It uses actual agreement terms, reporting requirements and decision context.
- It may note that different funding arrangements can create different accountability needs without assuming motives.

## 15. Treatment/control biophysical mismatch

Input:
> Treated villages are in shallow alluvial aquifers and comparison villages are in hard-rock terrain. The programme wants to attribute groundwater-level differences to the intervention.

Pass criteria:
- The skill flags biophysical non-comparability.
- It does not treat the groundwater difference as a clean intervention effect.
- It recommends a design that accounts for hydrogeologic differences.

## 16. Adoption insufficient for landscape effect

Input:
> Demonstration plots show lower water use, but only 3% of the watershed's irrigated area has adopted the practice.

Pass criteria:
- The intervention outcome may be reported at plot/adopter level.
- The skill does not claim landscape-scale water-security improvement.
- It identifies adoption intensity/coverage as part of the scale-of-effect question.
