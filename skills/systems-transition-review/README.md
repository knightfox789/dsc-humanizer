# DSC Systems Transition Review

A reusable review skill for deciding whether a pilot, programme or strategy has a credible pathway to wider scale or systems change.

## What it does

The skill separates four common scaling pathways:
- replication / service delivery;
- production-system / incentive transition;
- collective-action / trust transition;
- hybrid programmes that combine them.

It then reviews:
- intended scale and system boundary;
- what the pilot has actually demonstrated;
- current state versus desired state;
- necessary and enabling conditions;
- external trends and programme additionality;
- the rate-limiting condition;
- institutional ownership and recurring finance;
- delivery ecosystem and resource realism;
- evidence gaps and assumptions.

It finishes with a scale-readiness verdict:
- **GREEN** — ready for controlled scale;
- **AMBER** — promising but conditional;
- **RED** — pilot success does not establish a viable scale pathway;
- **GREY** — insufficient evidence to judge.

## Core principle

**Pilot success is not the same as scale readiness.**

A pilot can work because of unusual subsidy, exceptional leadership, intensive facilitation or favourable conditions that do not survive expansion. The review therefore asks whether the operating model itself can plausibly become the larger change being claimed.

## Typical use cases

- donor concept notes and proposals;
- Governing Board strategy reviews;
- water-security and groundwater programmes;
- watershed and NRM scale strategies;
- agriculture and natural-farming adoption programmes;
- government convergence / institutionalisation plans;
- WUA, aquifer, cooperative or FPO collective-action programmes;
- pilot evaluation before expansion;
- audit of `scalable`, `systems change` or `institutionalised` claims.

## Quick use

```text
Use the systems-transition-review skill.
Review this programme for scale readiness. Classify the scaling pathway, distinguish what the pilot has demonstrated from what remains assumed, map current and desired system conditions, identify the rate-limiting constraint and institutional owner, and give a GREEN / AMBER / RED / GREY verdict with priority next actions.
```
