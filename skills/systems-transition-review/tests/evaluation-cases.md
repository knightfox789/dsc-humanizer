# Systems Transition Review — Evaluation Cases

Use these cases whenever the skill changes.

## 1. Successful pilot is not automatically scalable

Input:
> A 10-village groundwater pilot achieved strong participation because each village had a full-time facilitator and free monitoring equipment. The proposal says the model is ready for 1,000 villages using existing government staff, but no staffing or equipment plan is provided.

Pass criteria:
- The pilot result is acknowledged.
- Scale readiness is not assumed.
- The review identifies facilitation intensity and equipment financing as scale conditions.
- The government-staff assumption is flagged as unverified.
- Verdict is normally AMBER, RED or GREY depending on the rest of the evidence, not automatically GREEN.

## 2. Replication pathway

Input:
> A tested water-quality testing protocol uses standard kits, a two-hour training, a fixed reporting template and district-level quality checks. Unit cost, supply chain and trainer capacity are documented for expansion to 20 districts.

Pass criteria:
- The skill recognises a primarily replication/service-delivery pathway.
- It does not invent unnecessary systems-change requirements.
- It still checks delivery capacity, quality assurance, unit cost and local adaptation.

## 3. Production-system behaviour cannot be reduced to training

Input:
> Farmers were trained to shift from a high-water crop to a lower-water crop. Adoption remains low because the high-water crop has assured procurement and the alternative crop does not.

Pass criteria:
- The review identifies market/procurement conditions as a material system constraint.
- It does not prescribe more training as the main solution without evidence.
- It classifies the pathway as production-system/incentive or hybrid.

## 4. Collective action and trust

Input:
> An aquifer-management programme proposes one awareness meeting per village, after which farmers are expected to agree on pumping limits and comply voluntarily.

Pass criteria:
- The review identifies collective-action/trust requirements.
- It tests rule-setting, legitimacy, enforcement, negotiation and conflict-resolution capacity.
- It does not equate awareness with collective governance.

## 5. Current state versus desired state

Input:
> The programme wants farmers to reduce groundwater pumping by 25% but does not describe changes in crop choice, electricity incentives, irrigation services, market demand or community rules.

Pass criteria:
- The skill maps the missing current-to-desired-state conditions.
- It identifies relevant actor and system changes rather than only farmer behaviour.
- It does not claim that all listed factors must change if evidence does not support that conclusion.

## 6. Programme additionality

Input:
> A state subsidy for micro-irrigation is already expanding rapidly. A donor project plans to claim the full increase in district adoption as its system-change result.

Pass criteria:
- The review separates ongoing government-driven change from project contribution.
- It asks what remaining gap the project actually influences.
- It avoids attribution inflation.

## 7. Rate-limiting condition

Input:
> A natural farming programme has trained 5,000 farmers and developed local inputs, but the only buyer for the crop requires certification that farmers cannot currently obtain.

Pass criteria:
- Certification/market access is considered as a possible rate-limiting condition.
- The review asks who controls it and whether it is tractable.
- Training volume does not dominate the scale-readiness judgement.

## 8. Institutional ownership

Input:
> A dashboard has been built for district water planning. The donor-funded project will end in six months. No department has agreed to host, maintain or finance the system.

Pass criteria:
- The dashboard is recognised as an output, not institutionalisation.
- Missing host, maintenance responsibility and recurring finance are explicit scale gaps.
- The skill does not call the tool sustainable or institutionalised.

## 9. Scale through parallel local processes

Input:
> A canal reform programme covers 300 Water User Associations. Each association requires negotiation of local rules and conflict resolution. The proposal assumes one central team can directly facilitate all groups sequentially in one year.

Pass criteria:
- The skill identifies a collective-action pathway.
- It tests whether distributed facilitation capacity or parallel local processes are needed.
- It does not prescribe a specific staffing ratio without evidence.
- Time and transaction costs are treated as real design constraints.

## 10. Hybrid pathway

Input:
> A watershed programme includes standard farm ponds, crop-water advisory, market linkage for less water-intensive crops and village groundwater rules.

Pass criteria:
- The skill separates replicable infrastructure, production/incentive change and collective-action governance components.
- It avoids forcing the whole programme into one pathway.
- The scale strategy addresses dependencies across the components.

## 11. Rate-limiter outside project control

Input:
> A programme's scale plan depends on a state policy notification that has been pending for two years and is outside the implementing organisation's authority.

Pass criteria:
- The policy decision is identified as a possible make-or-break external dependency.
- The review distinguishes influence from control.
- The verdict reflects this unresolved dependency.
- The skill does not promise scale because advocacy meetings are planned.

## 12. Biophysical boundary

Input:
> Groundwater governance is planned village by village, but the aquifer crosses 40 villages and pumping in one cluster affects water levels elsewhere.

Pass criteria:
- The review questions whether the system boundary is hydrologically appropriate.
- It does not assume administrative boundaries are sufficient.
- It identifies implications for governance and monitoring without inventing hydrogeological facts.

## 13. False scale from exceptional leadership

Input:
> The pilot succeeded because a senior project manager personally resolved every inter-departmental issue. The scale plan assumes the same coordination quality across 20 districts but has no institutional mechanism.

Pass criteria:
- Exceptional leadership is identified as a non-replicable pilot condition unless institutionalised.
- The review asks what routine coordination mechanism would replace the individual.
- Local success is not treated as scale evidence.

## 14. Scale verdict with incomplete evidence

Input:
> The concept note states that the model can be scaled statewide but gives no pilot cost, staffing pattern, adoption evidence, institutional owner or implementation mechanism.

Pass criteria:
- The skill uses GREY or RED depending on whether the claim is unsupported versus contradicted.
- It identifies the missing evidence explicitly.
- It does not fill gaps with assumed government convergence.
